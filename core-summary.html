EDCX246 High-Yield Exam Revision Tables

Part 1: The Pillars of Sociology

Chapter 1: Social Class

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Pierre Bourdieu Cultural Capital, Habitus, Symbolic Violence Schools mistake inherited middle-class traits (language, tastes) for innate intelligence. Working-class students face "symbolic violence" when their culture is devalued.
Karl Marx Base & Superstructure, Correspondence Principle Schools are ideological apparatuses mirroring the capitalist workplace (teaching docility for manual labor, autonomy for elites).
Max Weber / Randall Collins Class/Status/Party, Credentialism Dominant groups artificially inflate degree requirements to protect elite professions from working-class entry (social closure).
Basil Bernstein Restricted vs. Elaborated Codes Schools operate in "elaborated codes" (context-free, abstract). Working-class students use "restricted codes" (context-dependent) and are penalized.
Paul Willis Counter-school resistance Working-class "lads" see through the meritocracy myth, but their active rebellion ironically seals their fate in dead-end manual labor.

Chapter 2: Race, Ethnicity & Indigeneity

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Frantz Fanon Colonisation of the mind, Epistemic violence Assimilationist schooling forces marginalized youth to internalize Eurocentric standards, causing psychological alienation and self-rejection.
David Gillborn Institutional Racism, Whiteness as policy Racism isn't just individual bigotry; it’s structural. "Color-blind" policies, standardized tests, and behavioral streaming protect white advantage.
Educational Criminology School-to-prison pipeline Zero-tolerance discipline and subjective suspensions disproportionately criminalize Indigenous and racialized youth, pushing them into the justice system.
Critical Policy Analysis The "Indigenous Problem" Deficit Official policies pathologize First Nations students for statistical gaps instead of critiquing the monocultural, underfunded structures of the schools.

Chapter 3: Gender

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Raewyn Connell Hegemonic Masculinity, Gender regimes Schools actively manufacture gender. Hegemonic masculinity (stoicism, physical dominance, heterosexuality) subordinates girls and non-conforming boys.
Judith Butler Gender Performativity Gender is not a biological essence but a stylized repetition of acts enforced by institutional discipline (e.g., uniforms, peer policing, bathroom segregation).
Moral Panic Sociology The "Failing Boys" discourse A reactionary panic blaming female teachers for male underachievement. It homogenizes boys, ignoring that class/race—not gender alone—drive the data.
Developmental Biology Neuroplasticity vs. Neuro-myths Refutes the idea of "male" (spatial/math) and "female" (verbal/empathy) brains. Brains develop based on social conditioning, not rigid dimorphism.

Chapter 4: Sexualities

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Adrienne Rich Compulsory Heterosexuality Heterosexuality is treated as the default institutional norm (forms, prom, curriculum), forcing queer youth into invisibility or compliance.
Queer Youth Advocates Minority Stress Constant institutional erasure and unpoliced microaggressions cause severe, disproportionate psychological distress and school avoidance for LGBTQIA+ youth.
Medical Sociology The Intersex Matrix Biological sex is not a binary switch (XX/XY) but a multi-stage developmental spectrum. Medicalizing/normalizing intersex bodies enforces rigid social binaries.
Political Sociology Section 28 / Safe Schools Panic Conservative lobbies manufacture moral panics around inclusive education to score political points, subordinating child safety to electoral strategy.

Part 2: Governance & Subjectivity

Chapter 5: Governance

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Michel Foucault Disciplinary Power, Docile Bodies Modern power controls people not through violence, but through routines, timetables, and spatial confinement that optimize bodies for productivity and compliance.
Jeremy Bentham The Panopticon An architectural model of unverifiable surveillance. Because students never know when they are watched, they internalize the gaze and self-police.
Foucault (Late) Governmentality Power governs "at a distance." Schools use self-reflection sheets and goal-setting to make students the willing entrepreneurs of their own compliance.
Surveillance Capitalism Digital Dataveillance LMS click-tracking, biometric scanners, and apps like ClassDojo extend panoptic surveillance into the domestic sphere via constant metric auditing.

Chapter 6: Subjectivity

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Nikolas Rose The Psy-Complex Psychology and psychiatry reframe structural school failures (e.g., boring curriculum) as individual, internal psychological defects of the student.
Peter Conrad Medicalisation of Deviance Classroom non-compliance is transmuted from "badness" into "sickness" (e.g., ADHD), allowing schools to medicate disruption rather than fix pedagogy.
Judy Singer / Nick Walker Neurodiversity Paradigm, UDL Neurological variations are natural biodiversity, not diseases. Shifts focus from "curing" the student to removing disabling instructional barriers (UDL).
Educational Bureaucracy The Diagnostic Dilemma A clinical label is required to unlock statutory aide funding, but it risks permanently fossilizing a deficit identity and lowering teacher expectations.

Chapter 7: Neoliberalism & Marketisation

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Hayek & Friedman Vouchers, Choice, Spontaneous Order Neoliberalism frames education as a private commodity. "Funding follows the student" forces schools to operate like competing businesses.
Stephen Ball Performativity, Audit Culture High-stakes testing replaces professional trust with data targets, leading to curriculum narrowing and the "fabrication" of school success.
Institutional Economics Horizontal Competition Neighboring public schools fight for enrollments, leading to "cream-skimming" (taking easy kids) and "shedding" (pushing out complex-needs kids).
Public School Advocates Residualisation As middle-class families use capital to flee to subsidized private schools, public comprehensives become dumping grounds for concentrated disadvantage.

Chapter 8: Pre-Adulthood

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Philippe Ariès The Social Construction of Childhood "Childhood" is not a natural fact but an institutional invention. (Exam caveat: Ariès's "miniature adult" history is flawed, but the sociological premise holds).
James & Prout New Sociology of Childhood Rejects viewing kids as passive "human becomings" waiting for adulthood; treats them as competent "human beings" with active social agency.
Administrative Psych Developmentalism Schools use chronological age-batching to create artificial statistical norms, unfairly pathologizing natural variations in biological maturation.
Youth Sociologists Prolonged Adolescence Credential inflation and gig-economy precarity trap physically mature youth in extended states of financial dependency and institutional infantilization.

Chapter 9: Datafication

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Bob Lingard Governance by Numbers NAPLAN allows governments to steer schools "at a distance" via comparative league tables, without needing direct physical bureaucratic intervention.
Nerida Spina Accountability Washback The pressure of public data walls distorts pedagogy: teachers triage "bubble" students, drill test formats, and abandon non-tested subjects (Arts, Humanities).
Sociology of Subjectivity Metric Subjectivity Students internalize their value as a number. Teachers suffer "moral distress" when their relational vocation is reduced to a spreadsheet KPI.
Equity Reformers Diagnostic vs. Punitive Data While performative data is toxic, objective baseline metrics are essential to prove structural disadvantage (e.g., Gonski Review) and secure equity funding.

Part 3: Cultural Contexts

Chapter 10: The Media

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Stuart Hall Encoding/Decoding, Active Audience Rejects the "hypodermic needle" (brainwashing) myth. Audiences actively decode polysemic texts via Dominant, Negotiated, or Oppositional lenses.
Stanley Cohen Moral Panics, Folk Devils Media orchestrates disproportionate hysteria (e.g., failing standards, unruly youth) to trigger "deviancy amplification spirals" and justify regulatory crackdowns.
Political Economy Ideological Deflection Panics about "woke teachers" or "falling literacy" are deliberately deployed to distract the public from chronic state school underfunding.

Chapter 11: Popular Culture

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
John Fiske Semiotic Democracy, Textual Poaching Youth are not passive consumers of "rubbish" pop culture. They actively repurpose corporate commodities to build subcultural identities and resist adultism.
Julia Kristeva / Barthes Intertextuality, Polysemy No text exists in isolation. Youth media (memes, TikToks) relies entirely on a "mosaic of citations." Meaning is made by the reader, not the author.
Social Realist Educators Bridging to Powerful Knowledge Teachers shouldn't pander using fake slang. Instead, leverage students' pop-culture fluency as a bridge to teach rigorous semiotic critique and high-status literacy.

Chapter 12: Technology

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Marc Prensky (Critiqued) "Digital Native" Fallacy The dangerous myth that kids naturally understand tech. It masks the digital divide (poverty) and excuses schools from explicitly teaching critical online evaluation.
Neil Postman Media Ecology, Faustian Bargain Tech is never additive; it changes the whole environment. "Technology giveth and taketh away" (e.g., granting access but destroying sustained attention).
Shoshana Zuboff Surveillance Capitalism EdTech platforms (LMS, Google Classroom) are not free public services; they extract "behavioral surplus" data to monetize students for corporate algorithms.
Mike Oliver Social Model of Disability, UDL Tech's best use: Assistive tech (screen readers, dictation) removes disabling structural barriers, shifting the defect from the child to the environment.

Chapter 13: Globalisation

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Rizvi & Lingard Global Policy Field, Convergence National education systems are losing sovereignty, forced to adopt identical neoliberal reforms (vouchers, high-stakes testing) to compete in the global market.
OECD / World Bank Soft Power, PISA Shocks Non-elected bodies control policy via "governance by numbers." PISA league tables trigger media panics, forcing nations into a narrow STEM/literacy arms race.
Critical Global Pedagogy Beyond Cultural Tourism Rejecting superficial "food and flags" multiculturalism. True global literacy requires teaching supply chains, climate justice, and colonial legacies.

Part 4: Philosophy, Law & Foundations

Chapter 14: Philosophy

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Socrates / Classical Elenchus, Epistemology/Ontology Philosophy is not a luxury; it underpins all practice. Dialectical questioning inoculates students against passive compliance and political dogma.
John Dewey Pragmatism, Learning by Doing Rejects Cartesian dualisms (mind/body). Education isn't preparation for life; it is life. Schools must operate as embryonic democratic communities.
Paulo Freire Banking Model, Conscientisation Dismantles oppressive teaching where facts are "deposited" into passive students. Champions problem-posing dialogue to trigger liberating "praxis."
Humanistic Educators Eudaimonia vs. Human Capital Rejects neoliberalism's reduction of children to GDP economic units. Education must aim at holistic human flourishing and critical citizenship.

Chapter 15: Ethics & Law

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Kant / Bentham / Aristotle Deontology / Utilitarian / Virtue The ethical tension: Don't treat kids as means to an end (Kant) vs. sacrificing high-needs kids for overall school test scores (Utilitarian hazard).
Carol Gilligan / Noddings Relational Ethics of Care Rejects cold, rule-bound justice. Morality in teaching is grounded in attentiveness, responsiveness, and trust between the "one-caring" and "cared-for."
Common Law Courts Non-Delegable Duty of Care Replaces in loco parentis. Schools owe a non-transferable duty to prevent reasonably foreseeable physical and psychiatric harm.
McClellan Commission Statutory Safeguarding The legal floor vs. ethical ceiling. Mandatory reporting and Child Safe Standards were built because informal "trust" allowed horrific institutional abuse.

Chapter 16: Children's Rights

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Eglantyne Jebb / UN UNCRC (1989), Inalienable Rights Children are not parental property or passive welfare objects; they are sovereign rights-bearers entitled to dignity regardless of behavior.
Human Rights Jurists The "3 Ps" Framework Provision, Protection, Participation. Schools easily provide protection, but systematically suppress Participation because it challenges adult authority.
UNCRC Drafters Articles 28 & 29 Art 28 mandates dignity in discipline (no degrading punishment). Art 29 demands holistic development, which neoliberal curriculum narrowing violates.
Jenna Gillett-Swan Tokenism vs. Student Voice (Art 12) Exposes how schools co-opt student voice into "ornamental" councils (picking prom colors) while denying them say over pedagogy, rules, or assessment.

Chapter 17: Truth & Postcolonialism

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Michel Foucault Regimes of Truth Truth is an effect of power. Schools validate specific Western discourses as "objective fact" while classifying alternative epistemologies as primitive.
Edward Said / Spivak Orientalism, Epistemic Violence Colonial schooling systematically silenced subaltern voices and erased Indigenous knowledge, forcing students to internalize Western superiority.
Patrick Wolfe Structure, Not an Event Settler colonialism relies on a "logic of elimination." Underpins the "History Wars" (truth-telling regarding frontier violence vs. colonial denialism).
Martin Nakata The Cultural Interface The contested space where Indigenous traditions meet Western disciplines. Students must navigate this to master academic tools without assimilating.
Michael Young / Delpit Powerful Knowledge (Social Realism) The counter-weight to radical relativism. Refusing to explicitly teach working-class/Indigenous students standard literacy (the "codes of power") traps them in poverty.

Chapter 18: Alternative Education

Key Thinker(s) Core Concepts High-Yield Exam Focus
Maria Montessori Prepared Environment, Normalization Proves factory-model schooling isn't natural. Child-scaled environments with self-correcting materials foster intrinsic concentration without coercion.
A.S. Neill Summerhill, Radical Democracy Voluntary attendance and equal voting rights (kids and adults). Prioritizes emotional resilience and civic responsibility over academic cramming.
Illich & Holt Deschooling, Learning Webs Critiques the school credential monopoly. Proposes decentralized peer learning and natural unschooling immersion in the adult world.
Critical Sociologists The Paradox of Privilege Alternative schools often devolve into fee-paying enclaves for affluent families, leaving under-resourced public schools to manage high-needs cohorts.
