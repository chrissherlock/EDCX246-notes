Taxonomy of Logical Fallacies and Argumentative Errors

A Comprehensive Structural Audit of Fallacy Types Identified Within Poststructuralist Educational Frameworks

The following taxonomy catalogues the structural logical fallacies and argumentative errors identified within totalizing poststructuralist and critical sociological frameworks. This directory classifies the formal and informal types of reasoning flaws that emerge when sociological deconstruction overreaches into biological denialism and methodological contradiction.

Primary Argumentative Errors

1. Fallacy of Division and Sweeping Generalization

Conflating administrative batching, institutional categorization, and bureaucratic organization with the complete social manufacture of biological and cognitive human development.

2. Straw Man Fallacy

Characterizing empirical sciences, developmental psychology, and psychometrics as crude, unbending biological determinism while ignoring modern interactionist developmental models.

3. Fallacy of Privileged Ascription (Special Pleading)

Dismissing quantitative metrics and statistical baselines as corporate or state surveillance while treating qualitative discourse interpretation as infallible, unmediated truth.

4. Slippery Slope and Equivocation

Equating basic organizational routines, attendance tracking, and duty-of-care supervision with totalitarian carceral control and physical coercion.

5. Genetic Fallacy

Dismissing rigorous instructional frameworks, explicit literacy methods, and diagnostic baselines solely because they emerged within industrial modernity or institutional policy frameworks.

6. False Dilemma (Bifurcation)

Forcing an absolute binary choice between critical sociological fatalism and uncritical technocratic compliance, ignoring pragmatic pedagogical syntheses.

Additional Structural and Epistemic Errors

7. Begging the Question (Petitio Principii)

Assuming the conclusion within the premise—presupposing that all institutional structures are inherently systems of class reproduction before conducting empirical analysis.

8. Nirvana Fallacy (Perfectionist Fallacy)

Comparing existing public educational institutions and standardized assessment baselines against an impossible, idealized utopian standard rather than realistic alternatives.

9. Ecological Fallacy

Inferring individual psychological or cognitive characteristics solely from macro-level sociological group statistics and institutional trend data.

10. Reification (Hypostatization)

Treating abstract sociological concepts (such as "the state," "whiteness," or "habitus") as concrete, acting agents possessing independent consciousness and malignant intent.

11. Poisoning the Well (Circumstantial Ad Hominem)

Preemptively dismissing objective psychometric data, cognitive science, or administrative policy arguments by attributing them entirely to ruling-class self-interest or positivist bias.

12. Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc (False Cause)

Assuming that temporal succession or institutional correlation proves direct causal domination—attributing complex student behavioral challenges exclusively to school-based carceral governance.
